Before You Go

Bring These 5 Things

Most Important Rule

Attempt the problem before asking for help.

Tutors can help far more effectively when they can see where your understanding stopped.

Good Questions To Ask

Question #1 "Can you check my setup?"
Question #2 "Why does this logarithm property apply here?"
Question #3 "How do I know when to use logarithms?"
Question #4 "Can you show me another example?"
Question #5 "How would this appear on an exam?"
Question #6 "What mistake am I making repeatedly?"

Questions Specific To Chapter 5

  • Why is the domain of a logarithm always restricted?
  • How do I recognize when to use the Product Rule?
  • How do I know whether to expand or condense?
  • When should I use logarithms to solve an equation?
  • Why can't some exponential equations be solved with logs?
  • How do I find half-life and doubling time?
  • How do I convert between abt and aekt?
  • How do I interpret pH and decibel problems?

If You're Completely Stuck

Tell the Tutor These Three Things

  1. What chapter you're working on.
  2. What you understand so far.
  3. The exact step where you became confused.

After the Session

Do Not Leave Yet

Immediately solve one similar problem without looking at notes.

Being able to follow an explanation is not the same thing as being able to solve a problem yourself.

Confidence Strategy

Most students gain confidence in Chapter 5 through:

  • Repeated practice
  • Reviewing mistakes
  • Explaining solutions out loud
  • Working similar problems
  • Getting feedback in the Math Lab

Confidence comes from solving problems, not from rereading notes.
